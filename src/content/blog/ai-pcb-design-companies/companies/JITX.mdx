JITX seems pretty interesting. Founded in Berkeley, CA in 2017, their aim is to
build the infrastructure around which AI builds circuits (shocker). They've
raised around \$12 million in 2 rounds, with the latest being in 2022.

## Product

At its core, JITX is a representation of schematics and PCBs, a python library
to generate/interact with them, and an MCP. You connect the MCP to the AI tool
of you choice, and it generates code that uses the library to represent the
circuit. You can then feed it into Ansys for simulations.

They have started making that a bit of a loop, where the AI generates code that
goes into Ansys, then takes the output to iterate on the design. That's the bulk
of what their [demo video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUVSwqZazik) talks
about now.

## What they do well

They have a demo video, which is ground for credit on its own. It's insightful
and goes through features of the app, and isn't too fast-paced, so you can
actually see what's going on.

The product itself seems pretty cool too. They don't have their own AI, they let
you use your own, opening paths for local AI or other providers or harnesses.
They also don't try to replicate things that established tools do already. They
have plugins for KiCad and Altium, and their "simulation" is really
communicating with Ansys, a known simulation platform. I actually really like
this.

## What they suck at

No test results, no accuracy reports, anything like that. It's cool that they're
not false-advertising, but they're not true-advertising either. Their demo video
has 5.8k views. I have made Five Nights at Freddy's videos with more views than
that. Seriously, come on.

Their library is also FOSS, but the circuits you make must also be FOSS and
shared with them, which is pretty stupid in my opinion. The only other option is
enterprise, which I don't like, because that's unfriendly to sole founders &
individuals.
