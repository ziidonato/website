Cadstrom was founded in Montreal in 2023, and raised a \$6.8 million seed round
in December 2024. Their product is "continuous AI validation for PCB design".
purple-gradient website says it's "proven on medical, industrial, and consumer
designs". Supports Altium, OrCAD, Xpedition, KiCad, and Fusion 360(?). Their
whole argument is "finding issues late is expensive," pretty much the same
argument as every other product here.

## Product

Unlike a lot of other products here, Cadstrom has a demo video showing it
catching problems like incompatible supply voltage, indeterminate operation,
wrong pin configuration, I2C addresses, there's a lot here.
[I actually recommend checking this out](https://www.cadstrom.io/demo), at least
as an example of what could be possible.

## What they do well

From that demo video, it looks like it does excellent derating and configuation
checks. Shows pin tables & possible configurations, I2C addresses, clock speeds,
etc. For catching those quick mistakes or stuff from not seeing the right thing
on the datasheet, this seems pretty solid.

## Missing features

For one thing, website design. AI fatigue has set over all of us, and this
website screams AI design. Purple gradient background, purple gradient
emphasized text, purple gradient highlight over the "Get Started" button, come
on guys. This is 2026 and human design is back in style.

There is also no mention of pricing. I assume once you create an account you can
probably see it, but I shouldn't have to do that. I'll bet I'm not alone in that
belief either.

They're also explicitly leaving out simulation and only focusing on design
intent, which while I understand wanting to be the master of one discipline
instead of mediocre at multiple, they're leaving money on the table. Some other
AI startup is going to snatch that field up, because a lot of these simulations
are just calculus problems. A lot of them aren't even calculus problems. They
can easily start doing EMI/F, PCB layout, thermals, etc. allowing them to catch
a lot of other issues.

## Bad marketing

They have almost no online presence, meaning they're not advertising. That makes
sense if they're putting all the investment into the product, but that's not the
best business strategy all the time.
