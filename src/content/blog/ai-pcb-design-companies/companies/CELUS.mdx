CELUS is a German firm founded in 2016, with headquarters in Munich, Germany,
Porto, Portugal, and Austin, Texas. They have raised \$30 million in total, with
a \$25 million series A in 2022. Their main product is an "AI chatbot to PCB"
workflow, and they name Altium, Microchip, Siemens, NXP, and other semiconductor
players as their customers. From their about page, "We started CELUS because the
best part rarely wins." They discuss how engineers gravitate toward previously
verified parts instead of choosing new alternatives. I don't see how an AI
assistant helps qualify a part enough for an engineer to trust it _over a known
component_, but I digress.

## CELUS Agent

Their sole product, the CELUS Agent, aims to be the decisive "chatbot to PCB"
app that we've been conceptualizing. On their home page, they talk about their
flow (no video demonstration or anything):

1. The assistant asks questions "until the design intent is complete and nothing
   is left to guesswork"
2. It builds the solution, emphasizing "Trusted reference designs are matched to
   your requirements"
3. Gives you the files you need to continue working in your ECAD of choice.but

It seems like they're selling integration to component manufacturers as well,
allowing them to put their parts in the catalog and have them be suggested by
the AI. They list NXP as a customer among others.

## Concerns

They don't have an extensive database. Since their whole app runs on "CUBOS"
(blocks of functionality), if the AI can't match your specification to a certain
CUBO,
[their first suggestion is to "relax your requirements"](https://www.celus.io/knowledge/why-couldnt-i-find-the-solution-i-was-looking-for#:~:text=Try%20relaxing%20the%20specifications%20on%20the%20block%20and%20resolving%20again.).
Try saying that to someone making a machine to pinpoint some sorry sap at
mach 5.

I also don't see anything about testing. No results on accuracy, boards without
a mistake, anything like that. It looks like they're selling to manufacturers
though, so maybe they're not looking for serious board designers. All in all,
this seems like a company for part makers to get their parts in designs, whether
they're quality designs or not. Which is weird, because their
[terms of use](https://www.celus.io/legal/terms-of-use-celus-design-platform#:~:text=consumers%20in%20terms%20of%20Section%2013%20BGB%20are%20not%20eligible%20for%20registering%20as%20an%20End%20User)
says "consumers... are not eligible for registering as an End User."

That, combined with
[their explicit statement of them being able to publicize your designs if you miss a fee](https://www.celus.io/legal/terms-of-use-celus-design-platform#:~:text=3.4%20CELUS%20reserves%20the%20right%20to%20downgrade%20a%20Professional%20Account%20to%20a%20Community%20Account%2C%20if%20End%20User%20is%20in%20default%20of%20any%20fees%20due.),
with no mention of pricing anywhere on the site, I would stay away if I was a
serious designer.

They have virtually no online presence either,
[with their subreddit having one post from 2022](https://www.reddit.com/r/celus_io/).
