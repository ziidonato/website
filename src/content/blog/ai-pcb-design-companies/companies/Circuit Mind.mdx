Circuit Mind sells "The AI platform for electronics design." With a parent
company in Delaware, offices in London and Switzerland (where the CEO/CTO
reside), and a lot of UK Shareholders, CircuitMind is definitely more of a
"global" company.

## products

### Automated Circuit Engineering (ACE)

This is their main schematic generator. From the short demo on their
[platform page](https://www.circuitmind.io/platform), you can set up block
diagrams, dial in tradeoffs by percentage (not sure how that works), and chat
with an assistant. They then show the resulting schematic in Altium Designer.
[From the CEO](https://www.designing-electronics.com/ai-driven-embedded-power-design/#:~:text=The%20core%20algorithms,this%20leap%20possible.),
the way this works is by having an AI analyze datasheets and make "digital
representations" with power curves, decoupling, etc.

### Agentic Verification Engine (AVE)

On the review side, they have their agentic verification engine. This also
compares with data extracted from the datasheet, and allows organizations to
build their own rules to be checked at this time.

## What they do well

I realize that demo videos really help give off at least some legitimacy, even
if it's a bit fast-paced and unpausable where you can't see anything (like
theirs).

They're also (seemingly) honest about their numbers. In a paper by
[Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://5659512.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/5659512/LA-UR-25-30842_Rev1.pdf)
they show how much work the AI actually did for specific sections, and notes the
time saved. It says the verification engine saved 160 hours of work. The notes
of that section says "each respin causes a 1 month delay" which is true for
boards with a fatal mistake, but I don't see anything for boards without such a
mistake.

## Concerns

No public pricing, meaning they probably tailor it to their clients, and it's
probably expensive. The short demos don't really tell you much either, like it's
pushing you to email them if you want to know more. Also, no marketing. How do
they expect a tool to take off if they don't market it?
