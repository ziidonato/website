I've been into cars since the day my cousin put a controller in my hand and
raced me in Need for Speed: Carbon, and proceeded to beat me in every race all
around the map, with just about every car. At first, I liked the control, being
able to take a rather unwieldy car and control the over/understeer through the
corners, rocketing past anyone who managed to keep up with you (anyone who drove
muscle cars in that game knows what I'm talking about).

That initial spark developed into a passion for cars in general, then later on
the mechanisms within the cars, like what made a Japanese import feel different
from an American car, why they ended up that way, why Europe uses flat plane
crankshafts while American cars use cross plane cranks, what consequences that
has on the resulting product, what a rotary engine is, etc. Fun fact: before
software engineering, I was seriously considering mechanical/petrol engineering,
so I could start working with cars professionally, making those decisions.

So when I was in college, when I had the time to join a club, I immediately
gravitated towards Formula SAE once I discovered it. We get to build our own car
and race it against other institutions from around the world? ICE and EV? I had
to join. I walked into a meeting, met some people there, and they placed me on
the EV electrical/IC electrical teams. My first board was a shutdown relay relay
board, which monitored the status of various systems in the car and shut off
power to the motor if anything went awry. Next, I designed a PCB to help drivers
monitor the car, featuring an LCD screen to show things like oil/tire
temperatures, oil pressure, and lap times once we had our data acquisition
sensors installed. It also featured LEDs to show the driver the current RPM.

Once that was finished, we decided to start working on a damper dyno, a tool we
could use to reliably set the damping force of our car's suspension. The concept
was that we would tune it by hand until the driver was satisfied, then measure
the force. Once we had that number, any of our drivers could switch into the car
and we knew exactly how to calibrate the suspension to their preference.

I learned an incredible amount of engineering best-practices and standards at
the time, like how people work with composites to make lightweight parts, how
aerodynamic modeling and simulation is used to make engineering decisions, how
parts are designed in CAD to reduce manufacturing costs, not even mentioning the
incredible amount of experience in electrical engineering and troubleshooting
that I gained working on the electrical teams. My only regret is that I didn't
do it all 4 years.
